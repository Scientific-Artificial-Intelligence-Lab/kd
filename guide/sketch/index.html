    Complete a partial equation

    A sketch states what is already known about an equation before the search starts: terms whose coefficients are fixed, terms that must appear with coefficients fitted from the data, and room for a bounded number of unknown terms. Model.fit(sketch=...) passes it to the algorithm, and the result reports whether the selected law satisfies every clause.

    Write a sketch

    kd.Sketch takes three keyword clauses, any of which may be omitted, but not all of them:

    import kd

data = kd.load_burgers()
sketch = kd.Sketch(
    pinned={"u*u_x": -1.0},
    holes=[kd.Hole(max_terms=1, max_deriv_order=2)],
)
model = kd.Model(algorithm="sga", generations=50, seed=0).fit(data, sketch=sketch)
outcome = model.result_.sketch_outcome
    • pinned maps a term to its fixed coefficient. The pinned terms are subtracted from the regression target before the search and restored with their exact coefficients afterwards. A pinned coefficient must be finite and nonzero.
    • anchored lists terms that must appear in the law; their coefficients are fitted from the data, for example anchored=["u_xx"].
    • holes lists kd.Hole objects. A hole admits between min_terms (default 1) and max_terms terms beyond the pinned and anchored ones, each with a total derivative order of at most max_deriv_order, built only from the operators in operators and only from the fields in fields. A constraint left at None is not checked. Operators are named as in the law representation ("mul", "recip", "n2", "n3"; division is {"mul", "recip"}), and an empty collection admits only terms without operators. Several holes in one sketch need disjoint fields.

    With pinned terms, model.best_expr_ holds the expression the algorithm searched, without them; model.result_.equation and the outcome's laws include them.

    Term notation

    A term string is a name (u, u_xx), a product (u*u_x), a positive integer power (u**2, the product u*u), or KD's function-call notation (mul(u,u_x), diff2_x(u), n2(u)), and these forms can be mixed. n2 and n3 are separate operators that clip their input, so n2(u) and u**2 are different terms. Spellings of the same term, such as u_x*u and mul(u_x,u), are one term. A fixed coefficient is the value of a pinned entry and the constant column is the name one; sums, infix division, unary minus and ^ are refused, and add(...), div(...) and neg(...) remain available in function-call notation. The notation is described under Equation representation.

    Resolution against a dataset

    kd.Sketch called with these keywords returns a kd.SketchSpec, which holds the term strings, the pinned coefficients and the holes without reference to a dataset. Model.fit and kd.harness.run_episode resolve it against the dataset they fit: the left-hand side, the vocabulary (every field and axis of the dataset) and an exact match policy are taken from the dataset unless they are given. spec.resolve(dataset) performs the same step and returns the complete sketch, which is part of the run's identity for checkpoints and resuming. Each term is checked against the dataset's fields and axes when the sketch is resolved; a term that cannot be evaluated raises and names its clause, for example anchored[0] or pinned['u*u_x']. Resolution computes nothing on the data, so one spec can be resolved against several datasets.

    Algorithm support

    A sketch applies to evolution data and to tables. On a kd.TabularDataset, including an ODE system converted with kd.data.trajectories_to_tabular, the left-hand side is the target column, the vocabulary is the feature columns, and the intercept is the term one. A hole on a table cannot bound the derivative order, and a feature name that contains an underscore is refused. A law fitted on a table is compared with the sketch term by term: products and the powers n2 and n3 of sums are multiplied out, the result is split at its additions, and numeric factors become part of each coefficient, so x1*(x2 + 0.5) is the two terms x1*x2 and x1.

    On a table, a product counts as one term regardless of how its factors are grouped, so mul(x1,mul(x2,x3)) and mul(mul(x1,x2),x3) are the same term. n2 and n3 of a sum are counted by their monomials only when the law is compared with the sketch; the certified law keeps the fitted n2 or n3 term, so its input is still clipped.

    Each mode of each algorithm declares, clause by clause, how far it enforces a sketch, in the sketch block of its entry in kd.instrument_schemas(). Platform and plugins shows that block and the meaning of its levels. A sketch that uses a clause the selected mode does not support raises at fit(), before the search starts.

    Read the outcome

    model.result_.sketch_outcome holds four parts:

    • solution, the certified law. It is set only when the selected law satisfies every clause and fits the data within the verify policy of the fit; by default that policy is kd.SKETCH_EXIT_VERIFY, which requires an NMSE of at most 0.05.
    • best_candidate, the best law found, whether or not it was certified.
    • verdict, the clause-by-clause match of the selected law.
    • failure, the reason when no solution was certified.

    A certified solution means that the clauses and the residual condition hold for that law on the fitted dataset. fit(..., verify=kd.VerifyPolicy()) removes the residual threshold; a verification that cannot be computed still withholds the solution.

    Save and load a sketch

    Save the specification above as a JSON file, then load it for another fit:

    kd.save_sketch(sketch, "burgers-sketch.json")
loaded = kd.load_sketch("burgers-sketch.json")
model.fit(data, sketch=loaded)

    An unbound specification keeps its term strings and constraints; fit resolves them against the dataset. To bind the file immediately, pass dataset=data to kd.load_sketch. A complete sketch also records its left-hand side and vocabulary, which must match the dataset used later.

    Use a sketch with kd-agent

    After installing and configuring the controller, pass the same file through --sketch:

    kd-agent run --data burgers --sketch burgers-sketch.json "Find the missing diffusion term."

    The controller binds the sketch to the loaded data. Its selected search mode must support the requested clauses, just as with Model.fit. This command uses the configured language-model endpoint.

    Worked example

    The Burgers sketch script pins the advection coefficient, searches for the remaining terms, and reads the clause-by-clause outcome. On gridded evolution data, DISCOVER's PINN mode also accepts sketches: pinned terms enter its residual with fixed coefficients and anchored terms with fitted coefficients. EqGPT's multi-case wave and steady modes refuse sketches. On tables, PySINDy and LLM4ED accept the supported tabular clauses; consult the mode's schema before selecting other combinations.
